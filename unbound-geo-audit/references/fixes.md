# Turning a finding or a cited page into a fix

A fix has four parts: the page, the action, the exact words, the check. If you cannot name the page, you have a hunch, not a fix. Say so and go read the evidence.

Priority: fix pages the answers already cite before creating new ones. Engines reuse sources they have already leaned on for the question.

## From a finding type

**competitor_absent** (rivals named, brand absent). Open the questions where the rival was named. Find the cited pages for those questions in `list_cited_sources`. The fix is almost always on one of those pages, see the page types below.

**source_gap** (a cited page that leaves the brand out). The page is the target. Identify its type and use the matching section below.

**misdescription** (brand named, described wrong). Find the wrong fact in the evidence. Look where engines read it: your own About and pricing pages, your listings on review platforms and directories, the most cited page that states it. Correct the fact at its source. Write the right sentence in plain words on your own site first, for example "Acme is a payroll tool for companies of 5 to 50 people in the US. It does not offer invoicing." Then change third party listings to match word for word.

## By page type

### Listicle ("best X for Y", "top 10")
- Who: the author named on the page, or the site's editorial or partnerships address.
- Send: two lines on what you do, one differentiator they can verify, one link, a free account or a demo. Offer a fact they can use, such as a price, a customer count you can prove, or a feature the list lacks.
- If the list is sponsored, say so in the report. Do not describe a paid slot as earned.
- Template: "Hi <name>, your <title> comes up when people ask <question>. <Brand> does <one verifiable thing> for <who>, and it is not on the list. Here is a free account and our pricing page <url>. Happy to answer anything you want to verify."
- Check: the page lists the brand, and the next scan marks the page yes.

### Review platform (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot style)
- Page: the brand's own listing. Claim it if unclaimed.
- Do: fill every field with the same one-sentence description as your site, pick the exact category the cited comparison pages use, upload current pricing, email five real customers asking for an honest review with a link to the profile.
- Never buy or write reviews. Never ask for five stars.
- Check: the category page and comparison pages now include the brand.

### Directory or database (Crunchbase, product directories, local listings)
- Page: your listing. Make name, one-line description, URL, category, location and founding facts identical to the site.
- No listing yet: submit through the directory's own form and note the review time it states.
- Check: the cited directory page shows the brand with the correct description.

### Forum or community thread (Reddit, Stack Exchange, niche forums)
- Do not post as a fake customer. Do not create accounts to answer the thread.
- Do: one real team member replies once, discloses the affiliation in the first sentence, answers the original question with specifics, and links only if the forum allows it. Opening: "I work at <Brand>, so weigh this accordingly. For <the asked situation>, here is what we see..."
- If the thread is old or locked, publish a page on your own site that answers the same question in the same words, and link to it from docs and the help center.
- Check: the thread or your answer page shows up in `list_cited_sources` next scan. Forum citations swing a lot, so expect noise.

### Video (YouTube and similar)
- Who: the channel owner, through the contact in the channel's About tab.
- Ask for: a mention with a link in the description, or a short segment. Offer an account, a data point or an interview.
- On your own channel: title with the question as asked, the answer in the first 30 seconds, the full transcript in the description or on a page.
- Check: the description or transcript names the brand.

### News or editorial article
- Who: the byline, or the publication's tips address. Pitch a fact, not a company: a dataset, a result, a change.
- Never ask a journalist to "update" an article to add you. Ask whether a follow up fits.
- If the article is wrong about you, email the editor with the correct fact and a source they can open.
- Check: the correction or follow up exists and gets cited.

### Own site
- Page: the one that should answer the question. If it does not exist, make it, one question per page.
- Write: the question as the heading, a two sentence direct answer first, then specifics (who it is for, price, limits, how it compares). Say plainly what you do not do.
- Add: an About page with the factual one-liner, an FAQ built from real questions in `list_prompts`, Organization schema with the same name and URL. Confirm robots.txt lets crawlers in.
- Check: the page is cited, or the misdescription disappears, in the next scan.

## Sizing the effort

- Own site edit: minutes to hours, you control it.
- Listing edit: hours, plus their review time.
- Outreach: a week to hear back, and many will not reply. Send to three pages, not thirty.

## What not to write

"Improve your content", "build authority", "get more mentions", "optimize for AI". Each is a topic, not a fix. Replace with page, person, words, check.
